1. Introduction
Heritage tourism in India faces a persistent challenge — tourists and visitors at historical sites often have
no access to accurate, real-time, and location-specific information about the structures they are viewing.
Raigad Fort, situated in the Raigad district of Maharashtra, is one of the most significant forts in Indian
history and served as the capital of the Maratha Empire under Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj. The fort complex
contains multiple distinct historical structures including bastions, temples, gateways, and memorials, each
with deep cultural and architectural significance [1]. Despite thousands of visitors annually, the fort lacks
any intelligent digital guide infrastructure.
Visitors currently rely on generic physical signboards, printed pamphlets, or general-purpose applications
that provide no site-specific context. Tools like Google Lens perform broad object identification but are not
optimized for fine-grained recognition of closely related heritage structures within a single fort complex.
The visual similarity between structures such as the Maha Darwaja and Hirkani Buruj, combined with the
absence of publicly available labeled datasets for Raigad Fort, makes accurate image-based identification
a technically significant computer vision challenge [2].
With advances in deep learning and Generative Artificial Intelligence, it is now possible to build intelligent
systems that can identify specific historical locations from photographs and automatically provide rich,
contextually relevant historical information. Such systems not only improve tourist experience but also
serve as digital preservation tools for cultural heritage sites [3].
This project proposes HistTwin AI — an intelligent application that allows users to upload a photograph
of any structure at Raigad Fort, receive accurate identification of the location, and immediately access
detailed historical information about that specific place. The name HistTwin AI reflects the system's core
philosophy: creating a digital AI twin of the historical site that mirrors and conveys its historical identity.
The system uses ResNet50-based Transfer Learning for image classification and integrates Google Gemini
for AI-powered historical information generation [4].
1.2 Research Gaps
Analysis of existing work reveals several important gaps that HistTwin AI is designed to address. Most
current monument or heritage recognition systems use generic image search or broad object detection
models that are not trained on domain-specific or site-specific datasets. This causes failures when visually
similar structures within the same complex need to be differentiated [1], [2]. There is no existing publicly
available labeled dataset for the distinct structures of Raigad Fort, which means models trained on broader
heritage datasets perform poorly in this specific context.
While several studies have applied CNN-based transfer learning to monument recognition, they focus
purely on producing a classification label and do not integrate any information delivery pipeline [3], [4].
Once a location is identified, existing systems provide only a static class name with no associated
historical context, descriptions, or answers to visitor questions. There is a clear and practical gap in
systems that combine accurate visual recognition with dynamically generated, location-specific historical
information, which limits the usefulness of recognition systems for real-world tourism applications.
Many heritage recognition pipelines are designed as isolated classification engines and are not deployed
as interactive, user-facing web or mobile applications. The absence of a real-time REST API backend and
interactive frontend means these systems cannot be practically used by tourists at actual heritage sites [5],
[6]. A deployed, accessible application is essential for translating research accuracy into real-world impact.
Furthermore, most systems do not provide multilingual support, which is a significant barrier for regional
tourists in Maharashtra who may prefer to receive information in Marathi or Hindi rather than English.
Existing systems also lack a robust dataset quality validation pipeline. Training on corrupted, duplicate, or
low-resolution images significantly degrades model performance under real-world conditions [7], [8].
Without automated integrity auditing, classification outcomes cannot be fully trusted in field deployment.
1.3 Contributions
This paper addresses these gaps through the following contributions:
1. Domain-Specific Dataset Curation and Auditing:
We collected, organized, and programmatically audited a dataset of 990 labeled images across six
classes specific to Raigad Fort. A custom Python auditing module using MD5 hashing and PIL
image verification ensures data integrity, eliminating duplicate and corrupt images before training.
2. Two-Stage Transfer Learning Classification Model:
We present a ResNet50-based classification model trained via a two-stage Transfer Learning
strategy. Stage 1 focuses on feature extraction with the base model frozen, and Stage 2 performs
fine-tuning over the top 125 layers with a reduced learning rate. This achieves approximately
91% validation accuracy on five Fort location classes.
3. AI-Powered Historical Information Generation:
HistTwin AI integrates Google Gemini (via the OpenRouter API gateway) to generate dynamic,
multilingual, and engaging historical information for each identified location. The system delivers
comprehensive historical narratives, architectural details, and cultural significance in a
conversational tour-guide style, supporting English, Hindi, and Marathi.
4. Conversational Question Answering for Identified Locations:
Beyond a basic narrative, HistTwin AI allows users to ask specific follow-up questions about the
identified location. The system answers these questions using the Gemini LLM in context-aware
mode, providing an interactive and personalized information experience.
5. Full-Stack REST API and Web Application Deployment:
HistTwin AI is deployed as a FastAPI RESTful backend serving a responsive HTML/CSS/JS
web frontend. The backend supports image-based recognition, follow-up question answering,
and contextual text queries — enabling a complete conversational location guide experience.

2. Related Work
2.1 Monument and Landmark Recognition Using Image Processing
Image-based landmark recognition systems have been studied using various computer vision techniques
including feature matching, histogram analysis, and contour-based detection. These classical approaches
demonstrated that visual features like color, texture, and edges could be used to distinguish structures.
However, classical methods are sensitive to changes in lighting, viewpoint, and weather conditions,
significantly reducing their performance in real outdoor heritage environments [1].
Early image processing systems required manual feature design, leading to high complexity and limited
scalability. As noted in several studies, these methods do not generalize well to closely similar structures
that share many visual properties, such as different bastions or gateways within the same fort complex [2].
2.2 Transfer Learning for Heritage and Monument Classification
Transfer learning has emerged as a dominant approach for visual recognition tasks where labeled data is
limited. Pre-trained CNN models such as ResNet, VGG16, and MobileNet trained on ImageNet are
fine-tuned for specific classification tasks, allowing models to learn domain-specific visual patterns
without requiring millions of training images [3], [4].
Amato, Falchi, and Gennaro demonstrated that fine-tuning ResNet-based models on architectural
monument datasets significantly improves recognition accuracy over training from scratch, even with small
and imbalanced datasets. Similarly, work on Indian monument classification using MobileNet achieved
approximately 88% classification accuracy, showing the feasibility of Transfer Learning for heritage site
recognition tasks [5]. However, none of these works targeted site-specific fine-grained recognition within
a single fort, nor did they couple recognition with a live information delivery system.
2.3 Deep Learning Approaches for Fine-Grained Visual Recognition
Convolutional Neural Networks have shown strong performance in fine-grained visual classification —
distinguishing visually similar categories such as bird species, car models, or, in this case, closely related
historical structures. Attention-based CNN models proposed by Fu, Zheng, and Mei improve performance
by focusing the model on discriminative regions of images, which is valuable when distinguishing
structures like Jagadishwar Temple from Takmak Tok that may appear visually similar at a distance [6].
ResNet50 specifically benefits from residual skip connections which prevent vanishing gradients during
deep training and allow effective fine-tuning of high-level features. Despite high accuracy, these models
often require careful regularization, class balancing, and augmentation strategies to generalize well under
real-world photographic variations such as camera angle, zoom, and ambient lighting conditions [7].
2.4 AI-Powered Information Systems for Tourism and Heritage
Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) such as Google Gemini have created new
possibilities for tourism information systems. LLMs can generate contextually rich, historically accurate,
and engaging descriptive narratives based on location-specific prompts. Chen, Liu, and Wang demonstrated
that LLMs significantly improve the quality and personalization of heritage site information delivery
compared to pre-written static content [8]. The ability to handle follow-up questions and provide answers
in multiple languages makes LLMs an ideal backend for conversational tourist guide applications.
However, risks of hallucination in LLM-generated historical content remain a challenge that requires
cross-referencing with authoritative heritage documentation such as Archaeological Survey of India
records and established historical references to validate factual accuracy [9].
2.5 Smart Tourism Systems and Interactive Guides
Patel and Desai demonstrated that combining image recognition with NLP-based conversational question
answering in a mobile guide system improved tourist engagement by 60% compared to static information
boards. Their work highlights the value of context-aware, interactive guidance over static one-time outputs
[10]. However, their system was not designed for site-specific fine-grained recognition within a single
heritage complex, which limits its applicability to environments like Raigad Fort where multiple closely
similar structures coexist.
Existing smart tourism systems also generally lack multilingual support for regional Indian languages.
Given that a significant proportion of Raigad Fort visitors speak Marathi or Hindi, multilingual capability
is a critical practical feature for any tourism AI deployed in the Maharashtra heritage context [11].
3. System Architecture
HistTwin AI is an intelligent heritage recognition and information system designed to provide tourists
at Raigad Fort with immediate, AI-powered identification of historical structures and dynamically
generated location-specific historical information. The system is built as a modular, layered architecture
with clearly defined components that interact through a REST API backend.
The overall architecture consists of the following main components: image input module, data
preprocessing and augmentation pipeline, ResNet50 feature extraction engine, classification and
confidence thresholding module, Gemini LLM information generation module, and the interactive web
frontend.
3.1 Input Module (Image Upload and Query)
The user uploads a photograph of a Raigad Fort structure via the web-based frontend. The frontend
supports image-only input, image with a textual follow-up question, and text-only follow-up queries based
on a previously identified location. Input images are accepted in JPG, PNG, and JPEG formats. The
system uses FastAPI's UploadFile handler to securely receive and temporarily store uploaded images for
processing.
3.2 Image Preprocessing Module
Once uploaded, each image is resized to 224 x 224 pixels to match the ResNet50 input dimensions.
Raw pixel values in the range 0-255 are passed through ResNet50's built-in preprocess_input function,
which performs mean subtraction and RGB to BGR channel conversion consistent with the pre-training
data format. The preprocessing is embedded directly within the model graph so that it is applied
consistently during both training and inference.
3.3 Feature Extraction using ResNet50 Transfer Learning
HistTwin AI uses the ResNet50 architecture pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset as its backbone feature
extractor. ResNet50 is selected for its proven performance on fine-grained visual recognition tasks and
its use of residual skip connections that allow gradients to flow effectively across 50 deep layers. The
pre-trained base model captures low-level features such as edges and textures in its early layers and
high-level semantic features in its deeper layers, both of which are valuable for distinguishing architectural
structures within a heritage complex.
3.4 Classification and Confidence Thresholding Module
The features extracted by ResNet50 are passed through a custom classification head consisting of a
GlobalAveragePooling2D layer, a Dense(128) ReLU layer, Dropout(0.5) for regularization, and a final
Dense(6) Softmax output layer for six-class prediction. A minimum confidence threshold of 60 percent
is enforced. Any prediction with a softmax confidence below this threshold, or any prediction yielding
the Unknown class, is returned as Not a recognizable place to the user, preventing false historical
associations with unrelated inputs.
3.5 Gemini LLM Information Generation Module
Once a location is identified with sufficient confidence, its class label is passed to the Google Gemini
LLM via the OpenRouter API gateway. A structured prompt is constructed requesting a comprehensive,
historically accurate, and engaging narrative covering the location's origins, architectural significance,
cultural importance, and key historical events associated with that specific structure. The language of the
response is configurable, supporting English, Hindi, and Marathi. When the user submits a follow-up
question, that question is incorporated into the prompt so that Gemini answers it in full context of the
identified location, enabling a conversational and interactive guide experience.
3.6 Output Module
The final system output returned to the user includes the predicted location name, the confidence score,
and the AI-generated historical information narrative. The information is displayed in a clean, user-friendly
web interface designed for accessibility on both desktop and mobile devices. Users can then continue
the conversation by typing follow-up questions about the identified location.

4. Historical Site Recognition and Information Delivery Methodology
This section describes the detailed methodology and workflow of the proposed HistTwin AI system,
covering dataset preparation, model design, training strategy, and historical information generation.
4.1 Data Collection and Dataset Preparation
HistTwin AI relies on a purpose-built dataset of images depicting five distinct historical structures at
Raigad Fort plus an Unknown class. Images were collected through targeted web scraping of tourism
websites, image search engines, and Maharashtra heritage documentation sources. The dataset was
organized into class-labeled directories with the following distribution: Hirkani Buruj (117 images),
Jagadishwar Temple (228 images), Maha Darwaja (139 images), Shivaji Maharaj Samadhi (143
images), Takmak Tok (183 images), and Unknown (180 images), giving a total of 990 images.
4.2 Dataset Auditing and Integrity Verification
A custom Python script (analyze_dataset.py) was developed to programmatically audit the entire
dataset before training. The script uses the hashlib library to compute an MD5 cryptographic hash for
every image file, enabling byte-level duplicate detection. The Python Imaging Library PIL Image.verify()
method is used to identify corrupt or truncated image files. Images with pixel dimensions below 50x50
pixels are flagged as low-resolution and excluded. The auditing pipeline generates a structured summary
report (dataset_analysis.txt) for each class, showing total, unique, corrupt, and small image counts
along with a duplication ratio warning when duplicates exceed 50 percent of a class's images.
4.3 Image Preprocessing and Augmentation
All images are standardized to 224x224 pixels. A data augmentation sub-network is prepended to the
model input pipeline and includes:
1. RandomFlip (horizontal) to account for left/right orientation variations.
2. RandomRotation (factor 0.2) to handle camera tilt and angle differences.
3. RandomZoom (factor 0.2) to simulate different viewing distances from structures.
These augmentations artificially increase training data diversity and significantly reduce overfitting
without requiring additional real-world image collection.
4.4 Two-Stage Transfer Learning Training
Training is performed in two distinct stages to maximize the benefits of transfer learning:
Stage 1 - Feature Extraction: The ResNet50 base model is fully frozen and only the custom
classification head is trained for 10 warm-up epochs using the Adam optimizer at a default learning
rate of 0.001. Balanced class weights computed using sklearn.utils.class_weight are applied to
counter class imbalance in the dataset. This stage establishes an accurate classification head without
disturbing the pre-trained base weights.
Stage 2 - Fine-Tuning: The top 125 layers of ResNet50 (from index 50 onwards) are unfrozen and
the full model is trained end-to-end for up to 25 epochs using the Adam optimizer at a reduced learning
rate of 0.00001. EarlyStopping with patience 5, ReduceLROnPlateau with factor 0.2, and
ModelCheckpoint are applied as callbacks to prevent overfitting and save the best-performing model.
4.5 Prediction and Historical Information Generation
After training, the model is loaded once at module initialization to avoid repeated loading latency.
For each incoming image, the model performs Softmax prediction across six classes and applies
the 60 percent confidence threshold to filter uncertain predictions. Once a prediction is accepted, the
identified location class label is passed to the Gemini LLM with a structured prompt requesting a
comprehensive historical account of that specific location. The LLM generates a narrative covering the
historical origins, key events, architectural features, and cultural significance of the identified structure.
4.6 User Interaction and Conversational Support
HistTwin AI is designed to support multi-turn conversational interactions:
1. In image-only mode, the system identifies the location and returns a full historical narrative.
2. In image-plus-question mode, the user can ask a specific question about the identified location
   and receive a targeted, contextual answer from the Gemini LLM.
3. In text-only mode with context, the user can ask follow-up questions without re-uploading an
   image, maintaining conversational continuity across the session.
4. Language selection allows the user to receive the information in English, Hindi, or Marathi.
4.7 System Performance and Optimization
To ensure practical real-world performance, several optimizations are applied:
Use of ResNet50 Transfer Learning reduces time-to-accuracy compared to training from scratch.
The preprocess_input function is embedded in the model graph for consistent, efficient preprocessing.
Model is loaded once at application startup to avoid repeated disk read latency per request.
Balanced class weights during training compensate for dataset imbalance across fort location classes.
These optimizations make HistTwin AI practical and responsive in real-world deployment conditions.

5. Discussion
HistTwin AI demonstrates a practical and technically sound approach to solving the challenge of heritage
site recognition and visitor information delivery at Raigad Fort. By combining Transfer Learning-based
image classification with AI-powered location information generation, the system creates a useful and
accessible visitor experience that goes beyond what existing heritage recognition systems currently offer.
Key Observations:
 Accuracy and Classification Reliability:
The two-stage Transfer Learning strategy achieved approximately 91 percent validation accuracy on
the five-class Raigad Fort recognition problem. ResNet50's residual architecture effectively captures
discriminative visual features of architectural structures such as the distinctive archway of Maha
Darwaja, the dome of Jagadishwar Temple, and the cliff profile of Takmak Tok. Data augmentation
and balanced class weighting mitigated the effects of class imbalance in the training dataset.
 Historical Information Quality and Multilingual Support:
The Gemini LLM consistently produced historically contextual, factually grounded, and engagingly
written narratives for each identified location. Cross-referencing with Archaeological Survey of India
documentation confirmed factual accuracy for the primary historical claims. Multilingual output in
English, Hindi, and Marathi was validated and found suitable for general tourist audiences at the fort.
 Conversational Question Answering:
The system's ability to handle user-submitted follow-up questions about the identified location was
found to significantly improve visitor engagement. Users could ask specific questions such as the
historical significance of a structure, the legend associated with it, or the events that took place there,
and receive clear, contextual answers via the Gemini LLM pipeline without re-uploading an image.
 Practical System Usability:
The FastAPI REST backend handles three distinct interaction modes and returns structured JSON
responses that the web frontend renders in a clean, user-friendly interface. The conversational
multi-turn interaction design allows visitors to explore the history of a location through follow-up
questions without re-uploading images, mimicking the experience of speaking with a human guide.
 Limitations:
The current dataset covers only five Raigad Fort locations. The fort contains many more structures
including Raj Sabha, the Royal Kitchen, and multiple watchtowers that are not yet covered. Model
performance may degrade when photographs are taken from highly unusual angles, under extreme
lighting conditions, or when the structure is partially obscured or under restoration. LLM-generated
historical content requires cross-verification with authoritative sources to prevent factual inaccuracies.
 Comparison with Existing Systems:
Compared to existing monument recognition systems that only output a class label, HistTwin AI
delivers a complete information experience by coupling recognition with dynamic AI-generated
historical narratives and interactive question answering. This integrated approach represents a
significant practical improvement over systems that only perform classification without follow-up.
Summary:
HistTwin AI bridges the gap between academic AI research and practical cultural heritage tourism.
It delivers an accurate, intelligent, and informative visitor experience for Raigad Fort that is accessible
through any web browser. The system successfully demonstrates that combining deep learning-based
visual recognition with LLM-powered information delivery creates a meaningful and deployable
solution for heritage site tourism in India.
6. Conclusion
This paper presented HistTwin AI, an intelligent historical site recognition and location information
system for Raigad Fort, Maharashtra. The system addresses critical gaps in heritage tourism guidance by
providing: (1) Domain-Specific Visual Recognition: A fine-tuned ResNet50 classification model
achieving approximately 91 percent validation accuracy across five distinct Raigad Fort location classes,
enabled by automated dataset quality auditing using MD5 hashing and PIL image verification.
(2) AI-Powered Historical Information Delivery: Integration of the Google Gemini LLM to generate
contextually rich, multilingual, and historically accurate information for each identified location in a
conversational tour-guide style, supporting English, Hindi, and Marathi for diverse tourist audiences.
(3) Interactive Conversational Q&A: A question-answering system that allows users to ask follow-up
questions about the identified location and receive contextual, detailed answers from the Gemini LLM
without re-uploading images, creating a fluid and informative user experience.
(4) Full-Stack Deployable Application: A complete FastAPI REST backend and responsive web frontend
supporting multi-mode conversational interactions, enabling HistTwin AI to function as a real-world
24/7 digital information guide accessible from any device with a web browser.
The proposed framework establishes a foundation for scalable and intelligent AI-powered heritage tourism
systems. Future work should focus on expanding the dataset to cover all major Raigad Fort structures,
integrating AI-generated cinematic video reconstructions of identified locations for immersive visual
experience, adding text-to-speech for audio guide capability, and deploying on cloud infrastructure for
production-scale access. As India's rich heritage sites face growing pressure from digitization demands
and knowledge preservation challenges, intelligent AI systems like HistTwin AI will play an essential
role in making history accessible, interactive, and preserved for future generations.
